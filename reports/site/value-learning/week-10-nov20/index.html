1 · Both methods level off at 85–90%

Held-out success (330 matched trials, 95% intervals) against robot episodes used. AG counts its 900 base rollouts plus 100 per round. DSRL counts only its own episodes. Hollow markers redo round 6 from round 5 with a differently collected batch of 100 episodes (same budget as round 6), offset slightly so they don't overlap.

  • DSRL seed 1 goes 87.0 → 89.1 → 90.0 → 86.1 → 88.2% from 1,300 to 3,300 episodes. Its best checkpoint is 2.4 points above AG r5 (−2.4 to +7.3 paired), and training past 1,800 episodes adds nothing (3,300 vs 1,800: −0.9).
  • AG rounds after round 0 move within 74.5–87.6%. Re-running round 6 with more varied collection gave 80.3–84.2% against the original 77.3%. Only the random-push collection is clearly above the original (+7.0 [+1.5, +12.4]), and only +3.9 [−1.8, +9.4] against the plain rerun.
  • Re-evaluating the same checkpoint with a second noise seed moves r5 by −2.7 and r7 by −3.0. Two evals of the same policy agree on only 72–75% of trials.

2 · Guidance strength is not the bottleneck

Push = how far guidance moves the action, as a multiple of π₀.₅'s own sampling noise. Every round is calibrated to push 0.99. Left: push sweep around it. Right: small fixed weights (w = 1, 2, 4) against the calibrated weight (r5 w 9.8, r7 w 8.1).

r5r6r7
  • Push 0.8–1.2 is flat. Push 1.8 costs 9.7 (r5) and 11.5 (r7) points. Small fixed weights fall toward base: w = 1 gives 63–64% on all three rounds.
  • So the ~85% plateau does not come from too little or too much guidance; what limits success is in the learned head or its data.

3 · Distilling into π₀.₅ keeps the teacher's success

LoRA fine-tune of π₀.₅ (imitation, final checkpoint, sampled action targets) on the successful episodes of 300 rollouts from each teacher (train states). A distilled policy needs no residual head or DSRL actor at test time.

  • DSRL-distilled 88.2% (+35.8 [+29.7, +41.8] vs base), equal to the DSRL teacher on val (−0.9 vs seed 1 at 1,800). AG-distilled 89.1% on 266 of 330 trials (the eval stopped there), equal to AG r5 on the same trials (+0.8).
  • Distilling base's own successes gives no gain (49.7%, −2.7; 50.3% with the old executed-action targets, so the Nov 14 chunk-target fix does not matter here). The gain comes from the better teacher, and the fine-tune recipe (filtered BC round 1's) no longer loses points against base.
  • Hybrid collection (base for the first ~40% of the episode, then the DSRL actor) gives 80.9%.

4 · AG tolerates a noisy judge, DSRL does not

Each episode's success label flipped with probability 0.25 in both directions. Paired difference from the clean run at the same point.

  • AG round 0 trained on flipped labels: 76.1% vs 75.5% clean. DSRL with flipped rewards: 43.0% at 400 episodes and 35.2% at 600, below base. Its training success fell after the first 100 episodes.
  • For zima's VLM judge, this favours AG. The test uses symmetric, independent flips; a real judge's errors are likely lopsided and concentrated on near-misses.

5 · Changes that did not raise the plateau (and two that did)

Paired difference against the policy each variant is built on (same 330 trials, 95% bootstrap). Right of zero is better.

  • Moving the guidance reference away from frozen base does not help. Anchoring it to the data's average action costs up to 28 points. A learned no-tag branch peaks 7.9 points below standard guidance. Stacking on the previous round's policy is neutral.
  • IQL labels on round 7's data (−4.2) and on 100 base episodes (−15.8 at round 0) are worse than the value-binned labels.
  • Dropping the 29 demos helps. Round 0 on the 900 rollouts: +5.8 [−0.6, +11.8]. On 50 rollouts: +27.9 [+20.9, +35.2] (44.8 → 72.7%).
  • A 700-step limit instead of 520 adds nothing for r5 (+0.3): the remaining failures are stalls, not slow finishes.
