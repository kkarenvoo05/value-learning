Value learning for robot policies

Karen Vo · weekly progress

Can a small RECAP-style value function V(o) learn real task progress from robot camera images, and can it improve a policy? Weeks 5–6 worked on real-robot data (Mulligan Insert Marker). From week 7 the work moved to simulation (LIBERO) with π₀.₅, where V can be tested by fine-tuning the policy and running new rollouts.

Week 10 · Nov 20 update · current
LIBERO: AG and DSRL level off at 85–90%, distillation into π₀.₅ keeps it, and AG survives a noisy judge
DSRL seed 1 run to 3,300 episodes stays at 86–90%; AG r5 87.6%. π₀.₅ fine-tuned on DSRL / AG successes reaches 88–89%. With 25% of outcomes flipped, AG is unchanged and DSRL falls below base.
Week 9 · Nov 17 update
LIBERO: eight AG rounds, where the failures are, and the fixed-budget / continuous-state setup
AG rounds 0–7: 75.5–87.6% vs base 52.4%, no steady climb after round 0; the swings are real policy differences. Failures look random within each state, and what is left is mostly the second pot.
Week 9 · Nov 14 update
LIBERO: RL on top of π₀.₅ reaches 86–89%, how the remaining failures happen, and distilling it back
AG residual (round 5) 87.6% and DSRL (1,800 rollouts) 86.1% / 89.1% vs base 52.4%. Most remaining failures put the first pot on the stove edge. A chunk-target bug in our fine-tunes is fixed; its test is queued.
Week 9 · update for Nov 10, follow-up Nov 11
LIBERO: a fine-tune recipe that keeps base performance, and why the advantage tag goes unused
A fine-tune recipe that keeps base performance. Fine-tuning on our rollouts used to cost ~13–16 points. Making demos 75% of the training frames and using a 10× lower learning rate scored 53.6% vs base 52.4% (one seed; step 2000).
