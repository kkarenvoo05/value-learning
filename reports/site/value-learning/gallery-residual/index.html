Wrist view: what guidance changes at the grasp

The 224 px agent view is too small to see the gripper, so 30 trials (the 10 start states × repeats 0–2) were rerun with both policies, the same start states and noise seeds, logging the wrist camera, the gripper command, the finger opening and the pots' poses at every step. Matched-noise episodes are not bit-reproducible: base matched its gallery outcome on 25/30 reruns and guided on 17/30, so read this as how the two policies behave, not as an account of particular gallery trials.

  • Guidance removes a freeze after the grasp, not before it. Base often closes on a pot's handle and then holds still without lifting: for 3 s or more in 12/30 episodes, including 11 of its 15 failures. Guided never does (0/30). 6 of base's 47 lifts came 3 s or more after closing, against 0 of 56 for guided.
  • It is not faster or surer at grasping. Both reach and close on the first pot at about the same time (median step 118 vs 115), and guided does not need fewer attempts on the second pot (1.08 vs 1.20).
  • Guided fails differently. Of its 6 failures, 4 left a pot lying on its side (states 9, 13, 40, 42; base never did), mostly while placing a pot on the stove; 1 held still with open fingers at the second pot without closing, and 1 ran out of time. Open-hand holds of 3 s or more show up in 5 guided episodes against 2 for base. Tipping on placement is the one failure that plausibly comes from committing harder; the open-hand hover is base's own failure, which guidance does not remove.
  • Small numbers: 30 pairs, 6 guided failures. The tip and hover counts need more reruns to be firm.
