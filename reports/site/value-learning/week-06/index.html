Week 6 · Oct 20 – Oct 26, 2026

Mulligan Insert Marker (real-robot data)

Ablations and Lars's Oct 23 follow-ups: robot state, backbones, crops and architectures gave no gain, augmentation helped a little, and stacking a 2 s-old frame gave the first clear win on stalls. The week ended with an advantage-label check and a draft handover package for Lars.

From Oct 17 to Oct 26 we built and tested a small RECAP-style value function on Mulligan's real-robot Insert Marker data: two camera images in, a predicted 'how well is this going' score out. It learns real progress (it beats every trivial predictor, recognises a seated marker, and within a single round ranks eventual successes above failures increasingly well) but is limited by an ambiguity that one frame cannot resolve: a robot stuck at the holder looks like one about to insert. Most changes (cameras, robot state, augmentation, backbones, crops, architectures) gave small or no gains. The one clear win, stacking a 2 s-old frame with the current one, works as a stillness detector. The project then moved to LIBERO simulation with a frozen π₀.₅ backbone: testing whether a value function actually improves a policy requires new rollouts, which offline Mulligan data cannot provide, and the lab's goal is a self-improving π₀.₅.
Glossary: what the metrics mean
V (value)The model's estimate, from -1 to 0, of how well the episode is going: near 0 means about to succeed, below -0.5 means headed for failure.
TargetThe training label per frame: a straight countdown to the episode end, with an extra penalty if it fails.
Two humpsThe model outputs a probability over 201 value bins. These usually form a 'success' bump and a 'failure' bump, and V is their weighted average.
p_succProbability mass the model puts on the success side (values above -0.5).
CE (cross-entropy)The training loss, in nats. Lower is better; a difference of about 0.02-0.03 is the smallest this validation set can detect.
MAEAverage absolute gap between V and the target. Lower is better.
Shape MAEError after removing each episode's average level. It measures whether V's ups and downs within an episode are right. A flat line scores 0.037.
AUCHow well a score ranks one group above another: 0.5 is chance, 1.0 is perfect.
Within-round AUC at 1/5/10 sInside one robot round, how well V at that time ranks episodes that will succeed above those that won't.
stall_aucHow well p_succ separates success frames from frames where a failing robot has held still at the holder for at least 4 s.
Round / repoA batch of data collected with one policy version (r00-r04 autonomous evals; c00-c05 teleop or assisted). Rounds differ a lot in success rate.
Round-only constantA trivial predictor that outputs each round's typical training value. It is the strongest simple baseline.
StallA failed episode where the robot holds the marker at the holder without progress until it times out.
SeedsRepeated training runs with different random starts. Differences smaller than the seed-to-seed spread are noise.
Advantage labelRECAP's 'was this action better than usual' tag, computed from how V changes over the next n seconds.
Early vs late fusionCombining two frames' pixels before the encoder (early) vs encoding each frame and joining the features (late).
ProprioceptionThe robot's own state: gripper position, orientation and opening width.
baseline recipeThe baseline recipe: shared ResNet-18, both cameras, LeRobot-style augmentation, 3 seeds.

1. Adding robot state (proprioception) (Oct 20)

settled

Does the gripper's position and orientation help tell 'about to insert' from 'stuck'?

  • No gain on any metric across 3 seeds.
  • During the hold, stuck and about-to-seat episodes sit at the same gripper height (~230-250 mm), so position cannot separate them.
  • Image-only baseline kept. Video and state timing rechecked first: aligned with lag 0.

2. Why V looks random before the last ~10 s: it picks between two outcomes (Oct 20)

settled

Lars noticed V looks reasonable near the end but random earlier. Why?

  • The model predicts two humps, one for 'will succeed' and one for 'will fail', and V is their average.
  • About 62% of the error is putting weight on the wrong hump, not a bad value inside the right one (MAE 0.117 as predicted vs 0.045 with the right hump).
  • The worst cases are look-alike pairs at the holder: long stalls called successes, and late seats called failures.
  • Overconfident on the success side: at p_succ about 0.80, only 56% actually succeed.
Source: PROGRESS.md Stage 5 'Worst-case analysis'

3. Frame-to-frame flicker and the inference recipe (Oct 20; recipe Oct 26)

settled

Can the jitter be removed, and what is the best way to read V at use time?

  • Almost all jumps (R² 0.97) are flips in the balance between the two humps on near-identical frames.
  • A 0.5 s moving average cuts frame-to-frame change about 4x without hurting accuracy.
  • Best offline recipe: average 3 seeds plus centred 0.5 s smoothing (val CE 3.630 vs 3.672, flicker -77%). For live use, the trailing-average version scores 3.656.
  • Temperature scaling adds about nothing and was dropped.

4. Frozen encoder and a stronger backbone (DINOv2) (Oct 20-21)

settled

Is the image encoder the bottleneck? Would a frozen one or a bigger pretrained one help?

  • Freezing the ImageNet encoder is much worse (MAE 0.234 vs 0.121), so fine-tuning the encoder matters.
  • DINOv2 ViT-S/14 never beat ResNet-18 across three learning-rate recipes. It overfits earlier, and lowering its learning rate only slows that. Ruled out.

5. Image augmentation (Oct 19-22)

settled

Does augmenting training images reduce the flicker or improve accuracy?

  • Flicker: no. The best arm cut it by only 3%, so the jitter is not overfitting to pixel details.
  • Accuracy: yes, modestly. Crop jitter and Lars's LeRobot augmentation each lower val loss by about 0.06-0.07.
  • Best single recipe: crop jitter on the wrist camera, LeRobot augmentation on the side (CE 3.647, MAE 0.112).
  • Cost: every augmented arm separates outcomes worse at 1 s (AUC 0.60-0.63 vs 0.67).

settled

Were the hand-picked camera crops wrong, and does showing more of the frame or more pixels help?

7. Memory for stalls, first tries: late-fused history and 'seconds still' (Oct 22-25)

settled

Would showing V a past frame, or telling it how long the gripper has been still, fix the stall-vs-success confusion?

  • A past frame 2 s or 4 s back, encoded separately and joined late, gave no measurable gain.
  • Even a direct 'seconds the gripper has been still' input gave only about -0.016 val loss, which is not significant.
  • Main reason: many successes also freeze. 26% of successful episodes pause 8 s or more before seating, so stillness is a weak clue.
  • Later note (Oct 27, 10 seeds): the still-time gain shrank to -0.007.

8. Stacking the current and a 2 s-old frame into one image (Oct 26) - first win

settled

Does feeding both frames' pixels together into the encoder (early fusion) catch stalls that late fusion missed?

  • Yes. This is the first memory change to pass the win rule: val CE 3.627 vs 3.672, every seed better than every baseline seed.
  • Ranking successes above still stalls jumps from 0.800 to 0.912.
  • It works as a pixel stillness detector: 'nothing changed in 2 s' lowers V and visible change raises it. It therefore also lowers V on successes that are paused.
  • Later checks (Oct 27): the same result on another GPU type. A 1 s gap is as good. A 4 s gap, 3 past frames, or hand-velocity input do not beat it.

9. Architecture: separate encoder per camera, attention fusion (Oct 24-25)

settled

Lars suggested different features per camera and a transformer that combines them. Does either help?

  • Two separate ResNets (one per camera) tie the shared one (CE 3.670 vs 3.672). Twice the parameters buys faster fitting, not better results.
  • Attention fusion is worse and noisier in all three variants. It overfits early even with a smaller head and dropout.

10. What each camera contributes, and wrist-camera dropout (Oct 26)

settled

Which camera does V rely on in each phase? Is the wrist view the source of V's optimism on failures?

  • In the last 5 s of successes the wrist camera dominates: removing it costs +2.2 to +3.0 nats per frame (it sees the seat).
  • On failures the side camera carries V, and the wrist view makes failures look slightly more hopeful. The pattern is the same in all three architectures.
  • Training with the wrist randomly dropped halved the model's reliance on it, but optimism on failures stayed at +0.155. The optimism comes from what both cameras show.

11. Advantage-label sanity check on Mulligan (Oct 26)

settled

If we turn V into RECAP 'good action / bad action' labels, do the labels make sense?

  • The labels point the right way and are not just a clock effect, but the signal is weak.
  • At the same round and time, frames from successes get 6.1 points more 'good' labels at a 2 s lookahead (13.7 at 5 s). Pauses before a seat beat stalls by 12.7 points.
  • Caution: 72% of frames at the end of failures are labelled 'good' (V was already low), so a decision on those is needed before policy training.
  • Ensembling plus smoothing cuts label flips from 2.1/s to 0.62/s.
