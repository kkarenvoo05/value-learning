1 · Round-1 drop: seed noise for b100, real for the ~50-base chains

Each chain retrained round 1 with a second training seed on identical data, labels and settings. Held-out success at push 0.99: val = 330 matched trials on 10 fixed start states, fresh = 300 new layouts. Lines join round 0 to each round-1 seed.

Round 0Round 1, seed 0Round 1, seed 1Base π₀.₅
  • b100 (100 base episodes, the 900-rollout V): seed 1 scores 77.0 / 78.0% against seed 0's 53.0 / 45.3%, within 3 points of round 0. Two retrains on the same data differed by 24–33 points.
  • b50cv (50 base episodes) drops to 10–15% with both seeds; the seeds agree within 2 points. mt100cv (50 moka + 50 task-64 base episodes) drops on moka with both seeds, but seed 1 holds on task 64 (73.3% vs round 0's 77.3%) where seed 0 fell to 33.3%.
  • Correction: the "4.5%" quoted on Nov 20 for mt100cv round 1 came from an eval with 280 client-errored episodes counted as failures. The rerun reads 32.1% (305 trials). Fixed in the Nov 20 report and page.

2 · Oversampling base episodes rescues the fixed states, not fresh layouts

mt100cv round 1 retrained on the same 100 base episodes, repeated so they make up a larger share of the training frames (seed 0; the hollow marker is seed 1 at the original share).

Round 1 at each base shareSeed 1, original shareBase π₀.₅Round 0 (solid line)
  • At 91% base frames, val returns to round 0 (64.2 vs 65.8%), but fresh moka layouts reach only base level (54.3 vs base 55.0) and task 64 stays well below it (46.0 vs 60.7). At 77%, nothing improves.
  • Karen's reading: the guided share a chain tolerates grows with its distinct base episodes; reweighting 100 of them cannot replace more layouts. Section 1 adds that seed noise is as large as most of these effects, which is why the factorial below uses two seeds.

3 · Offset G0: deliberate ~2 cm offsets create choices worth ranking

At 80 branch points (10 val states × 2 base episodes × 4 times), the simulator restores the state and runs 8 candidates 4 times each: the plain π₀.₅ chunk, and the same chunk shifted ~2 cm along ±x, ±y, ±z (or +1 cm in z). Left: success of the candidate that did best on one repeat, scored on the other 3, minus the plain chunk. Right: each fixed direction minus the plain chunk. 95% intervals bootstrap over base episodes.

Offset for 1 chunk (5 steps)Offset held 4 chunks (20 steps)
  • Picking among offsets beats the plain policy by 12 points in both probes. Picking among π₀.₅'s own noise samples (G0, Nov 4) gave +0.6, because those candidates end ~2 mm apart. The pre-registered gate passes.
  • No single direction helps in both probes. z+ (raising the hand, the hypothesis from the residual-failure analysis) does nothing. z− in the 1-chunk probe is the only interval above 0 and does not replicate. The useful offset depends on the state, so it takes a ranker, not a fixed push.
  • The +12 is an upper bound for a learned critic, since it uses the simulator's own outcome on a held-out repeat. Next per Karen's rule: G1, train Q(s, a) on data with these offsets and test whether it ranks them at held-out branch points.

4 · Round 1 does not re-apply round 0's push

The "double push" idea: round-1 data was collected with round 0's push, so round 1 might learn that push and apply it again. Here, each policy's push (guided minus base action, arm motion only) is measured on the same 192 val frames, and two policies' pushes are compared. Cosine per frame: 1 = same direction, 0 = unrelated. β: how much of the second policy's push the first contains. Preliminary: 10 of 17 policies scored so far (rerun of Karen's analysis on the finished ones).

Two round-0 policies (reference)Round 1 that kept val successRound 1 that dropped
  • The round-1 retrain that dropped hardest (mt100cv seed 0) is the least aligned with its round 0 (per-frame cosine −0.11, β 0.37), the opposite of what double pushing predicts. The 77% and 91% oversampled retrains point the same way as each other (0.31, 0.28) yet one drops and one does not.
  • Two round-0 policies from different chains agree more (0.54–0.58) than any round 1 does with its own round 0. Alignment does not separate the dropping retrains from the others in these 10.
  • In normalised units the gripper holds 50–68% of the push energy, concentrated at grasp and release; Karen scores arm motion separately for that reason.
